Helix App Studio: staff settings, store pipeline and troubleshooting

For ExpoPlatform's operations team: what staff set for a client's app and where, how an app goes from store set-up to a rollout and what the client sees at each step, and what to check when a client reports a message from App Studio.

What ExpoPlatform staff set, and where

Clients cannot change these settings in App Studio. The client guide says they are set by the client's ExpoPlatform team, and lists them on Set by your ExpoPlatform team. Unless an entry says otherwise, staff make them in the Helix admin, on the app's own page or on the Distribution page.

Store approval

  • Each app has the switch “Store publication needs our approval”. It is on by default, and staff change it on the app's Settings tab in the Helix admin.
  • When the switch is on, a new app's store set-up request and every publication request wait for staff on the store requests board. The board is on the Helix admin's Distribution page, under “Store requests from App Studio”.
  • When it is off, the set-up happens at once under ExpoPlatform's own publishing account, and Submit to the stores publishes at once: the client reads “Sent to the stores”.

Store set-up, publishing accounts and keys

  • Every app made in App Studio sends a store set-up request when it is created. Until staff approve it, the app cannot be built. An app with no set-up request, made before such requests existed or by staff, is never held.
  • Staff approve the request on the store requests board and choose the publishing account the app goes out under. Or they decline it with a reason, which the client reads in Build & test and Publish.
  • A publishing account is an Apple Developer team and a Google Play Console account. An app needs that account's App Store Connect key for iOS and its Google Play key for Android. Staff add the publishing account's store keys. Clients never choose the account, and Publish does not show its name: it reads “Our store account”.
  • Approving the set-up records the app for both stores under the chosen account, and starts its App Store Connect record. If that start fails, the request says so, and staff run the step from the app's store checklist. Google Play gives Helix no way to create an app, so staff create it in Google Play Console; that step stays on the checklist.
  • Staff then verify, run or mark the checklist's steps for each store record: 12 for an iPhone app and 13 for Android. Clients see the checklist but cannot change it; their one step is the demo account for the store reviewers.
  • For the iOS home-screen widgets, staff create the shared App Group with Run and check it with Verify on that checklist, or a signed iOS build creates it when it is missing. That step holds back neither the release nor the progress figure.
  • Each store record's publishing account, bundle id and ownership are on the app's Distribution tab in the Helix admin. Staff also set up an Android app's own upload key.

Rollouts

  • Staff start, pause, resume, halt, advance or speed up a store rollout, and prepare a fix-forward version, on the app's Distribution tab in the Helix admin. Clients only follow the rollout in Publish.
  • Which releases wait for staff, and which go out by themselves, is under The store pipeline below. The client guide tells clients that their ExpoPlatform team starts the rollout.

Update prompts and the side-menu logo

  • Staff set the app's update prompts: from which version people must update, from which they are asked to update, and the prompts' wording. Clients see the rules on their app's Overview, under App updates.
  • In the app, a version below Must update shows a prompt that cannot be skipped, and a version below Asked to update shows one that people may dismiss. The app checks when it starts and when it comes back to the foreground.
  • Staff also decide whether the app's side menu shows the ExpoPlatform logo at its foot.

Design style and tab bar

  • App Studio does not offer the design style, the tab bar style, or the switch that lets people pick their own style in the app. Staff set them on the app's Content & Design tab in the Helix admin, under Design. A client's Save changes keeps what staff set.
  • A new app made in App Studio starts on the design style and tab bar style ExpoPlatform chose for new apps, with that switch off. An app copied from another one takes that app's design.
  • The design style also decides whether the badge background and sponsor pictures show: App Studio notes they are “Shown by the Max design styles.”

Other settings only staff change

  • Google Play drafts: whether the app's Google Play releases wait as drafts in Google Play Console, and publishing those drafts. Meanwhile the client reads “Our team publishes this release in Google Play. It reads Live once the store confirms it.”
  • Test channels other than TestFlight and Google Play internal testing: Google Play closed and open testing, and TestFlight for the App Store Connect team. Their releases still show in the client's list of sends to testers.
  • Store review: sending the demo account to App Store Connect by hand, waiving the demo event check, and the review items App Studio marks Our team. When the check is waived, Publish says “The demo event content is not checked for this app.”
  • Sending the store listing to the stores: it goes out with a publication request when staff approve it. Filling the listing's pictures from the screenshots is done in the Helix admin; clients attach their own set from Store screenshots.
  • The AI assistant: whether it is switched on for the app, whether people may speak to it, and how much one attendee may use it. A client can only lower those limits.
  • Which languages the apps offer, and the standard wording that every app shares.
  • Recording and deleting feature videos. Clients can only watch and download them.
  • Service mode and clearing the whole home content. Service mode shows ExpoPlatform's feedback button on every screen of the app, to everyone who uses it, so staff switch it off before the app goes to attendees. It is on the app's Settings tab in the Helix admin.

Not in App Studio

  • Push notifications have no section in App Studio: they stay in the client's ExpoPlatform admin, and the client guide says so.

The store pipeline

Clients follow these steps in Build & test and Publish; staff act in the Helix admin. The client guide walks clients through them on From test build to the stores.

  1. Set-up. Creating an app sends the store set-up request. Until staff approve it, Build & test and Publish say “ExpoPlatform is setting this app up in the App Store and Google Play - you can build it once that is done”. After a decline they say “ExpoPlatform did not set this app up in the stores”, followed by the reason when there is one.
  2. Build. Only a build signed for the store can go to testers and to the stores. A build that could not be signed says “Not signed for the store: this app has no store key with us yet. You can still install it to try it. Contact us to add the key.”
  3. Test. The client sends a build to TestFlight or to Google Play internal testing, with no approval needed. App Studio tells them “Apple checks the build first, usually within a day. Then your testers get an email invite.” and “Ready for your testers within minutes, with no review.” An app takes one release at a time per platform, store releases included.
  4. Publication request. In Publish, Submit to the stores sends one finished, signed build per store. Each store with such a build starts ticked, with its newest build chosen. While approval is on, the client reads “Sent to ExpoPlatform for approval”, then “Waiting for ExpoPlatform approval: [builds], sent [when].”, and cannot send another request until staff decide.
  5. Decision. Staff approve or decline the request on the store requests board. A decline shows the client “Declined: [reason]”. An approval sends the store listing to the stores and creates and submits one store release per build; the client reads “Approved [when]: [builds] went to the stores' review. Follow it below.” If publishing cannot start, the client reads “Approved, but the stores could not take it yet. Our team is on it: [builds].” and the board marks the request “Approved, stores refused”.
  6. First release in a store. An app's first release in a store also needs that store's listing to be complete and sent to that store. Until the listing is complete, Publish tells the client “Complete your store listing”.
  7. Store review. A store release moves through Draft, Uploading, Processing, Ready, Submitted, In review and Approved. A Ready release is sent for review by itself. Each iPhone submission files the demo account in App Store Connect's review details, with ExpoPlatform Support as the review contact, and sets the app's App Store price to free. Without a demo account, the iPhone submission fails and the release stays Ready.
  8. Rollout. Apple releases an app's first iPhone version as soon as it approves it. An Android release goes out to a first 5% of people. Later iPhone versions, and Android beyond the first 5%, wait until staff start the rollout. While a rollout runs, Helix watches how often the app crashes, and on Android how often it stops responding, and moves the rollout on, holds it or stops it. A stopped rollout shows “The rollout is stopped” in Publish.
  • App Studio asks the stores for an app's live version at most once an hour. A version that has just changed in a store can take that long to show.
  • While an app's store listing in App Studio has no text and no picture, App Studio fills it from the live listing in App Store Connect and Google Play. A listing that already has any text or picture is never replaced.

Opening App Studio

Clients open App Studio from their own ExpoPlatform admin; App Studio has no sign-in of its own. Opened there, it stays signed in for up to 8 hours. A link or bookmark to App Studio's own address does not sign anyone in. The client guide covers this on Open App Studio.

The client opens App Studio again from their ExpoPlatform admin.

Not saved: [reason]

The change was not saved.

  • If an app's page in App Studio shows only a plain page saying it cannot be found, check that the client's ExpoPlatform admin lists the app for their account and that the app is not archived.

Troubleshooting

Each entry quotes the message as App Studio shows it, with a word in square brackets for a part that changes. Then it says where the message shows, what it means, and what staff check or tell the client.

Events & updates, Sign-in & registration, Menu & content

Your admin did not answer - try again
A red message or line in these three sections.
These sections need an answer from the client's ExpoPlatform admin, and it did not answer in time. The first open of one of them for an app takes longer, because it copies the old App Builder's settings (see below).
Check that the client's ExpoPlatform admin is up, then try again.
App not found
A red message or line in these three sections.
The app is not one the client can open in App Studio, or the client's ExpoPlatform admin does not know it.
Check that the client's ExpoPlatform admin lists the app for their account and that the app is not archived.
  • The first time Events & updates, Sign-in & registration or Menu & content is opened for an app, App Studio copies the old App Builder's settings into the app: its general settings and every event's settings and menu, in every language. That first open takes longer.
  • From then on the app uses App Studio's copy. A change made in the old App Builder no longer reaches the app: make it in App Studio. An event added later in the client's ExpoPlatform admin is copied at the next open of one of these sections.
  • Only the choice of events in Events & updates is also saved to the client's ExpoPlatform admin, and documents added in Menu & content are stored there. The client guide explains this on Your old App Builder settings.
